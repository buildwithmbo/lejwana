Rough and Polished

On yield: what a rough parcel really becomes in polished

Lejwana · April 2026

The least understood fact in the diamond trade, outside the trade, is how much of a rough diamond is lost on the way to polished. Most of the stone that comes out of the ground never reaches a ring. It becomes dust on the polishing wheel, offcuts from the saw, and weight sacrificed to symmetry. What remains is the yield, and yield is the entire economics of manufacturing.

Where the weight goes

A polisher looks at a rough stone and sees a set of competing outcomes. Cut for maximum weight and the proportions suffer, dragging the price per carat down. Cut for ideal proportions and weight is surrendered, but each remaining carat is worth more. Inclusions force further choices: plan around them, cut through them, or split the stone into two smaller ones. Every rough diamond is an optimisation problem with money on both sides of the equation.

Two identical parcels of rough, planned by two different hands, can produce meaningfully different value in polished.

Why buyers of rough get this wrong

A parcel of rough is priced on what it should become. If the buyer's yield assumptions are optimistic, the parcel is overpaid for before a wheel ever turns, and no amount of skilled polishing recovers the loss. This is why rough-to-polished modelling belongs before the purchase, not after it: estimate the recoverable polished stone by stone, price the polished against the current market, subtract the cost of manufacture, and only then decide what the rough is worth.

This modelling is one of the core things Lejwana's advisory work does. The house is run by a certified polisher and valuator, and its yield estimates come from the wheel, not the spreadsheet alone. For producers deciding how to sell and buyers deciding what to pay, the discipline is the same: know what the parcel really becomes, before someone else's assumption becomes your cost.
