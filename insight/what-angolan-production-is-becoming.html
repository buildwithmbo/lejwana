What Angolan production is becoming

Lejwana · June 2026

Angola is one of the world's significant diamond producers, and for most of its history that production has told a simple story: rough out. Stones recovered from Angolan ground were sold as rough, and the cutting, polishing and margin happened elsewhere.

The structure behind that story is state-led. Endiama, the national diamond company, sits over production. Sodiam, its trading arm, handles marketing and sales. Together they control how Angolan goods reach the market, and in recent years the direction they have set has changed: more transparency in how rough is sold, more openness to investment, and a stated ambition to build polished capacity in country.

The capacity being built

Cutting and polishing in Angola is early, but it is real, and it is policy rather than experiment. Works are being established, skills are being developed, and a share of production is beginning to be reserved for local manufacture. The pattern will be familiar to anyone who watched Botswana over the last twenty years: a producing state deciding that exporting rough alone leaves too much value on the table.

Angola is not a future source. It is a present one, changing shape.

What buyers should watch

Two things. First, supply: as local manufacture grows, polished goods of Angolan origin will come to market through new channels, and buyers with relationships in Luanda will see them early. Second, documentation: Angolan goods move under the Kimberley Process, and origin paperwork on polished from a state-controlled chain can be strong. The house's Angolan relationships exist for both reasons. Lejwana works with the supply network forming around Angola's new capacity, applying the same documentation standard it applies everywhere else.
