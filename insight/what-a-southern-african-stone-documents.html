On Provenance

What a Southern African stone actually documents: provenance, plainly

Lejwana · May 2026

Provenance is the most used and least explained word in the modern diamond trade. Every seller claims it. Few can say precisely what their paperwork proves. So here is what travels with a Southern African stone handled by this house, and what each document actually establishes.

The documents, one by one

The Kimberley Process certificate covers the rough. It certifies that the parcel crossed borders through the official scheme and is conflict-free under the scheme's definition. It is a floor, not a ceiling: necessary, and not sufficient on its own.

Country-of-origin paperwork goes further. It records the country, and where the supply chain allows, the mine, the rough came from. On goods bought through state channels or direct producer relationships, this record can be specific and strong.

The GIA report establishes what the polished stone is: its weight, colour, clarity, cut and measurements, graded by an independent laboratory to a standard the whole trade recognises. The laser inscription ties that report physically to the stone, so the certificate in the folder and the diamond in the parcel cannot quietly part company.

iTraceiT blockchain verification connects the chain. Each transfer of the stone is recorded, so the path from origin to current holder can be audited rather than asserted.

Provenance is not one document. It is the absence of gaps between several.

Why it matters commercially

Regulation, retailer sourcing policies and consumer expectation are all moving the same way: towards proof. A stone whose record is complete is easier to sell, easier to finance and easier to defend. A stone whose record has gaps carries a discount, whether or not it is ever spoken aloud. The house documents every stone fully because that is what the market is coming to require, and because a Southern African stone with its papers in order is the strongest provenance story in the trade.
