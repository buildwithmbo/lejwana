Journey: a stone from a Debswana parcel to a European buyer

Lejwana · March 2026

Provenance reads abstractly until you follow one stone. So here is the journey a Southern African diamond typically makes through this house, from a Debswana parcel to a buyer's desk in Europe, with every document it collects on the way.

Botswana: recovery to rough sale

The stone is recovered at a Debswana operation and enters the sorting and valuation process in Gaborone. When its parcel is sold, whether through the established sales channel or the Okavango Diamond Company's own sales, it moves with its Kimberley Process certification and its origin recorded. At this point the stone is rough, and its country of origin is already a documented fact rather than a claim.

The wheel: rough to polished

The parcel is planned and manufactured at a cutting works in Botswana. The planner maps the rough, weighs yield against proportion, and commits. Weeks later the stone exists in its final form. It travels to the GIA for grading, returns with its report, and is laser inscribed with the report number, tying certificate to stone permanently. Its record is entered into iTraceiT, where each subsequent transfer will be logged.

By the time the stone leaves Botswana, its history is already written down. Nothing after this point depends on anyone's word.

The corridor: Gaborone to the buyer

A European buyer briefs the house on a requirement the stone matches. The stone is offered with its full record: GIA report, inscription, origin paperwork, Kimberley documentation, blockchain trail. On confirmation, it ships under bonded, insured transport, customs clearance handled by the house, chain of custody unbroken from vault to delivery.

The buyer opens the parcel and holds a stone whose entire journey can be audited: mined in Botswana, cut in Botswana, graded independently, tracked at every transfer. That is what the house means by provenance. Not a story. A file.
