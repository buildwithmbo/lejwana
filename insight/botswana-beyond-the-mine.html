Botswana beyond the mine: beneficiation, ODC, and a national diamond strategy

Lejwana · July 2026

Botswana's relationship with diamonds is unlike any other producing country's. Diamond revenue built the state's schools, roads and health system, and the industry remains central to the national economy. So when Botswana sets diamond policy, it is not adjusting a sector. It is steering the country.

The policy has a name the trade should take seriously: beneficiation. The principle is that a stone mined in Botswana should, wherever possible, be cut, polished and sold with Botswana participation, so the value added between rough and polished stays in the country that produced it.

The two levers

Beneficiation works through two main levers. The first is manufacturing: cutting and polishing works operating in Botswana, employing and training Batswana, turning local rough into local polished. The second is sales. Through the Okavango Diamond Company, the state sells a share of Debswana production independently, giving Botswana its own window on the market, its own customer relationships and its own price discovery.

Beneficiation is not a subsidy or a sentiment. It is a producing country deciding to be a trading country as well.

What it means in practice

For buyers, Botswana's strategy creates something valuable: polished goods with the shortest possible provenance chain. A stone mined, cut and sold in Botswana crosses fewer borders and passes through fewer hands than almost any other diamond in the world. Its documentation reflects that.

Lejwana is a product of this strategy as much as a participant in it. The house was founded in Gaborone, is run by a certified Botswana polisher and valuator, and works with the local manufacturing base beneficiation created. When the house says Botswana is the gateway, this is what it means: the national strategy and the house's own model point the same way.
