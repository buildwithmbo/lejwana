What does provenance prove?

Lejwana · Trade explainer

What the paperwork shows, and what it does not.

Provenance is a record of where a diamond came from and who has held it since. At its strongest, it ties a stone to a mine or producer and to the documents it picked up on the way to market.

For rough, this usually means export and import permits and a Kimberley Process certificate, which states that a parcel has been handled under the scheme’s rules. For polished, it can include producer or manufacturer records and a grading report.

What it proves is limited. A certificate shows that the paperwork exists and is consistent. It does not by itself prove a diamond’s origin once rough from several sources has been mixed or cut. Many polished stones cannot be traced to a single mine, and a claim that they can should be supported by records, not by assertion.

For buyers, the useful question is how far back the records go, who vouches for each step, and whether the documents match the goods.
