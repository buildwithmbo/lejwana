From Botswana to the buyer

Lejwana · Trade explainer

The trade, logistics and documentation journey.

A diamond’s route from mine to buyer is a chain of handovers, and each one leaves a record.

It starts at the mine, where rough is recovered and sorted. In Botswana, a share of production is sold through the Okavango Diamond Company and the rest through De Beers contract sales.

From there, goods move under export permits, with a Kimberley Process certificate, to a sorting and sales centre, then to a manufacturer. Manufacturing may happen in Botswana, or in another cutting centre such as India.

Once polished, stones are graded and documented, then sold to dealers, jewellers and brand owners. Each move depends on insured transit, customs clearance and matching paperwork at both ends.

At Lejwana, this is the part we manage on behalf of buyers: confirming that the stones, the documents and the delivery terms agree before goods move.

For questions on this, write to us.
