Rough, polished and yield

Lejwana · Trade explainer

What a rough parcel really becomes.

A rough diamond is not a small polished one. Cutting removes most of the original weight, and the share that stays is called the yield.

Yield depends on the shape and quality of the rough and on what the manufacturer is aiming to produce. A well-formed crystal can give a high yield. A flat, included or irregular one gives far less.

This is why rough is priced by what it can make, not by weight alone. A manufacturer looks at a parcel and works backwards. They estimate the polished it will yield, the likely value of that polished, and the cost of getting there, then arrive at a price they can pay.

For a polished buyer, the point is simple. A polished stone’s price already carries the weight lost in cutting, the labour and the manufacturer’s margin. Understanding yield explains why polished prices move differently from rough.
