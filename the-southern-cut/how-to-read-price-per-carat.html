How to read price per carat

Lejwana · Trade explainer

Why one average can mislead.

Price per carat is the headline figure in rough and polished reports. It is also easy to misread.

A carat is a unit of weight. A price per carat is the total value divided by the total weight, so it is an average over everything in the sale. If a producer sells a different mix of sizes and qualities from one period to the next, the average will move even if no individual price has changed.

That is why a rising average can reflect richer goods and not stronger prices, and a falling one can reflect a larger share of smaller or lower-quality stones.

To read the figure properly, look for:

  • the assortment or mix being sold
  • the size ranges and quality bands
  • whether the figure is for rough or polished
  • the currency, and whether it is a list, auction or tender price

Comparing like with like, such as the same size and quality across two sales, tells you far more than a single average.
