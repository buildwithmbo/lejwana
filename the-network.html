The Network

Southern Africa.

Four producing countries. One regional network. Botswana as the gateway.

Botswana is Lejwana's gateway into Southern Africa. It is where the house was founded, where its London Diamond Bourse membership is anchored, and where its corridor relationships begin. From Botswana, Lejwana works across Angola, South Africa and Namibia.

Regional integration, on national terms

Each producing country runs its own diamond strategy. Botswana pursues beneficiation, keeping more cutting and polishing at home, with state supply channelled through the Okavango Diamond Company. Angola is building polished capacity behind Endiama and Sodiam. Namibia markets a share of its production through NAMDIA. South Africa operates a transformation framework across its diamond ecosystem.

Lejwana works within these national frameworks. Sourcing, cutting, placing and partnerships should support the source country's strategy rather than bypass it. That position is not a constraint on the business. It is the reason the network holds.

Country profiles

The gateway

Botswana

+

Botswana is one of the world's most important diamond producers by value, and the country where diamonds and national development are most closely tied. Production is dominated by Debswana, the partnership between the government and De Beers, with state sales through the Okavango Diamond Company giving Botswana its own route to market.

For Lejwana, Botswana is more than the largest source. It is home. The house was founded in Gaborone, its bourse membership is anchored there, and its relationships with local cutting and polishing works are the foundation the rest of the network is built on. Beneficiation is national policy, and it is also the house's own operating logic: value added in country, by people in country.

Producing country

Angola

+

Angola's production, led by state company Endiama and marketed through Sodiam, has historically left the country as rough. That is changing. Polished capacity is being developed in country, and a supply network is forming around it. Lejwana's Angolan relationships give buyers a route into that developing supply, with the same documentation standard applied as everywhere else the house works.

Producing country

South Africa

+

South Africa has the region's most established diamond ecosystem: mines, cutting works, dealers and a deep bench of trade skill. The sector operates under a national transformation framework, and working there means working within it. Lejwana sources and places goods through South African counterparties who meet both the framework's requirements and the house's own.

Producing country

Namibia

+

Namibia's production, much of it recovered from the seabed, is among the highest value per carat in the world. The state markets a share of it directly through NAMDIA. Lejwana's Namibian relationships cover this supply, connecting buyers to goods with origin documentation from a country that treats provenance as a national asset.

Adjacent market

Lesotho

+

Lesotho sits outside the core network but inside its reach. The Letšeng mine produces some of the world's largest and finest rough, and these trophy stones are accessed through corridor relationships rather than sourced on a standing basis. When a Letšeng-class requirement arises, the house knows where to take it.
